A Japandi style bedroom is low, quiet and warm: a low wood platform bed, linen in two or three soft tones, warm light from a lamp or paper shade, and almost nothing on the floor. A Japandi living room runs on the same rules, with a low sofa, a solid wood coffee table and closed storage. ## What is Japandi style?

Japandi is a mix of Japanese and Scandinavian design: warm minimalism, natural materials, low furniture and very few things. The name [emerged around 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japandi), and it draws on two ideas, the Japanese wabi-sabi (beauty in imperfect, handmade things) and the Danish hygge (feeling cozy at home).

The link is much older than the name. [Scandinavian designers and artists began visiting Japan in the mid-1800s](https://gessato.com/?p=325269), after the country opened its borders, and many came to admire Japanese craft and design.

In a real room, it comes down to seven rules:

- Warm minimalism: fewer things, but soft and used. Spare, never a white box.
- Natural materials: one main wood tone, from light oak or ash to walnut, plus a contrasting wood or black accent or two. Linen, cotton and wool for fabrics. Paper, ceramic and stone for the small things.
- Low furniture: beds, sofas and tables sit close to the floor, which makes the ceiling feel higher.
- An open floor: leave walking space around every piece. Bare floor is part of the look.
- Closed storage: drawers and doors, not open shelves full of stuff.
- Handmade over perfect: a lumpy ceramic lamp beats a glossy one.
- A few plants: one big plant with a strong shape, maybe two small ones. Not a jungle.

## What colors are Japandi?

Japandi colors are warm neutrals (warm white, greige and sand), earthy accents like clay and muted sage, and a little charcoal or black for contrast. Here's where each one goes.

| Color | Where it goes | How much |
|---|---|---|
| Warm white | Ceiling and most walls | The base of the room |
| Greige or sand | One wall behind the bed or sofa, bedding, upholstery | Plenty |
| Natural wood | Bed, tables, dresser | Plenty |
| Clay or terracotta | Cushions, a ceramic lamp, plant pots | Small touches |
| Muted sage | A throw, one cushion, the plants themselves | Small touches |
| Charcoal or black | Picture frames, a lamp base, a stool | One or two pieces |

Warm undertones matter more than the exact shade. Cool grey next to pale oak makes the wood look orange and the room feel cold. A warm white or sand wall picks up the wood instead.

Before you paint, put a sample patch right next to your floor or bed frame. Look at it at night under your own bulbs, not only in daylight.

## How to set up a Japandi style bedroom

Start with the bed, then take things away until the room goes quiet. These are the rules for a Japandi bedroom design:

- A low platform bed in light to mid wood. Skip the tall tufted headboard.
- One simple nightstand, or none. One lamp and one small object on it.
- Hidden storage: a dresser with drawers, closed boxes under the bed. Nothing lives on the floor.
- Layered bedding in two or three tones, such as white linen, a sand duvet and a chunky oatmeal knit.
- Low, warm light. A paper lantern or a ceramic lamp, with bulbs around 2,700 K. The US Department of Energy calls that [warm light that suits the wood finishes and fabrics in homes](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/purchasing-energy-efficient-light-bulbs).
- One large artwork per wall, or none. Skip the gallery wall: the bare wall around the piece is part of the calm.

## Japandi living room: layout and rules

A Japandi living room is built around one low sofa, one solid wood table and a rug that marks where you sit. Everything else is optional.

- A low sofa in linen or boucle, in oatmeal, sand or warm white.
- A solid wood coffee table, chunky and plain.
- One low armchair or a couple of poufs, not a matching set of armchairs. Fewer seats keep the view across the room open.
- One large plant with a strong shape, such as a fiddle-leaf fig, plus one or two smaller ones.
- Sheer curtains in linen or cotton, hung close to the ceiling.
- A rug big enough to hold the sofa and the table, so the seating reads as one zone.
- A low sideboard with doors for everything else.

## Is Japandi the same as Scandinavian?

No. Japandi is lower and quieter, with darker accents. Scandinavian is brighter and a little fuller.

| Feature | Japandi | Scandinavian |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | Warm white, sand, clay, sage, charcoal accents | Bright white, light grey, soft pastels |
| Wood | One main tone, light oak to walnut, plus black accents | Pale wood throughout |
| Furniture | Low, close to the floor, solid | Standard height, often on slim legs |
| Pattern | Almost none; texture does the work | Stripes and simple prints on cushions |
| Mood | Calm and spare | Bright and lived-in |

## Can I make a small bedroom Japandi?

Yes. Japandi suits small bedrooms well, because its main move is taking things out.

- Keep furniture low so your eye travels over it. A clear line at eye level makes the room read bigger.
- Pick a bed with storage underneath, or slide closed boxes under it, and drop the second nightstand.
- Paint the walls one warm white or sand so the corners blur.
- Hang a mirror beside or across from the window so it bounces daylight around.
- Use a wall light or a pendant instead of a floor lamp, to free up floor space.
- Keep all the textiles in one color family.

The attic bedroom above shows how much a small, awkward room can take. The dresser stands against the side wall, the low bed sits in the middle on a jute rug, and the floor around them stays clear.

:::tip{title="Got a sloped ceiling?"}
Paint the slope a warm white or sand, matching the walls, so the angle stops looking like a break in the room. Keep tall pieces on the high side and the low bed under the slope.
:::

## Start this weekend: what to change first

Do it in this order. Removing things costs nothing, and paint comes last because an empty room shows you what color it needs.

:::steps
1. **Clear the floor and every surface.** Put back only what you use every day.
2. **Swap the bedding or sofa throw.** Go for linen or cotton in white, oatmeal or sand, plus one chunky knit.
3. **Change the bulbs.** Use warm white everywhere and switch off the main overhead light in the evening.
4. **Add a paper shade and one plant.** A paper lantern over a bare bulb is one of the cheapest big changes you can make.
5. **Paint last.** Do one warm wall behind the bed or sofa, or the whole room in warm white.
:::

If you only get through the first three steps, the room will already feel calmer.

## Mistakes that make Japandi look cold or themed

Japandi usually goes wrong in one of two ways: too cold, or too much.

| Mistake | Why it fails | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cool grey walls and bright white everything | The room feels like an office | Warm white, sand or greige |
| All blond wood, all matching | It looks like a showroom set | Add one darker wood or black piece |
| Too many objects | The calm is gone | Keep a few things you love, store the rest |
| Fans, bamboo prints and other Asian-themed props | Style turns into a theme | Let paper, ceramic and wood carry the Japanese side |
| One harsh overhead light | Texture goes flat | Lamps at low height, warm bulbs |
| Smooth surfaces only | The room feels hard | Linen, knit, jute and rough ceramic |
