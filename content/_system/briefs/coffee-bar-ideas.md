## Intent

The searcher drinks coffee (or matcha) at home and wants a dedicated spot that looks good and works: where to put it, what to put on it, how to store things, and how to make it fit a small space. They install when they see an ordinary, awkward spot in a real home (a dead-end landing, a junk room, a plain corner) become a convincing coffee bar, and realise they can preview their own spot before buying a cart or cabinet.

## Value first (STYLE.md section 1 wins over anything below)

reader takeaway: the reader can plan and set up their own home coffee bar without the app. They know where to put it (power, water, space), the minimum counter and shelf sizes, the basic kit by drink type, storage that keeps the counter clear, how to style it so it doesn't look cluttered, and small-space options (cart, cabinet, wall shelves, cupboard station).

value sections (each must pass the remove-the-app test; the first H2 is one of these, pure value):
1. Where to put a coffee bar: a checklist of what the spot needs. A power outlet (an espresso machine plus grinder and kettle: mind circuit load), water nearby or a refill routine, a surface depth that fits the machine, and a place out of the cooking zone. Candidate spots: a kitchen counter end, a sideboard in the dining or living room, a hallway or landing niche, an unused corner, a closet or cupboard, under the stairs. Give practical measurements only where the guard can verify them (web-sourced; counter depth, shelf spacing for mugs/canisters). No invented numbers.
2. Coffee bar ideas by space, as the core list (H3s, roughly 8–12 ideas). Each idea gets 2–4 lines of concrete how-to: countertop station on a tray, a rolling cart, a sideboard/buffet bar, a cabinet with doors (hide it when not in use), floating shelves + mug rail, a pegboard wall, a cupboard/closet coffee station, under-stairs, a corner bar, a landing/hall niche, a coffee + matcha bar, a coffee bar with a mini fridge. **The app demos go inside this list** where they match (landing niche, junk room, corner).
3. "What do you need for a home coffee bar?" Answer in the first sentence. A table: core kit by drink style (drip/pour-over, espresso, matcha), with storage and extras (canisters, mug hooks, a tray, a small bin or knock box, a syrup/sweetener caddy, a bowl or whisk for matcha). No brand names.
4. Coffee bar ideas for small spaces: rules (go vertical, one tray as the boundary, wall hooks, a closed cabinet, keep only daily-use items out, one machine not three).
5. Styling without clutter: a 3-zone layout (make, store, serve), what to display vs hide, one plant, one print, lighting (a plug-in sconce or under-shelf light; no hardwiring advice).
6. Mistakes: no outlet nearby, a counter too shallow for the machine, everything on display, steam under hanging shelves or cabinets, a bar placed in the cooking zone traffic path.

where the app fits: inside section 2 (the ideas list), at 2–3 ideas, with a before/after of a real room, plus one `:::looks` or `:::style-slider` if the images allow it. Target about 70% reader help and 30% product.
